# Creative brief — Story 01 (source of truth)

> Verbatim brief for the first story of the Corporate Technology campaign. The storyboard,
> design system and code must not change the facts below, invent metrics, or invent
> technical functionality that is not listed here.

I want you to create a cinematic, story-driven internal campaign video for Elm's Corporate Technology sector.
This video should NOT feel like a technical explainer, a PowerPoint animation, or a traditional corporate case study.
The video should tell the story of ONE real employee and show his experience BEFORE and AFTER automation.
The employee is a junior IT Support employee.
He was dealing with a repetitive manual process for device delivery and asset registration.
The process required:

* Paper forms
* Physical signatures
* Repetitive emails
* Manual updates to System 800
* Follow-ups between employees and the Asset Team
* Records distributed across emails and files
* Repeating the same process across multiple sites

The process required significant manual effort and created opportunities for human error, missed emails, delayed updates, incomplete records, and fragmented documentation.
The employee experienced this problem personally every day.
Instead of simply accepting the process, he decided to improve it.
The most important part of the story:
He had NO previous automation experience.
He started learning independently, experimenting with available tools, and eventually built an automated workflow that transformed the process.
The new process enables:

* Digital submission
* Automated workflow triggering
* Review and approval/rejection
* Automatic generation of the required record
* Centralized record storage
* Consolidated data across multiple sites
* Notifications to the Asset Team
* Notifications to the employee
* Reduced dependency on manual emails
* Reduced dependency on paper
* Better process consistency and accountability

## CORE CREATIVE DIRECTION

The video should follow this storytelling structure:
PERSON → PROBLEM → FRUSTRATION → QUESTION → LEARNING → SOLUTION → BEFORE/AFTER → IMPACT.
The employee must be the HERO.
The automation technology is only the ENABLER.
The video should make the viewer feel the improvement instead of simply explaining it.

## VIDEO LENGTH

Approximately 60–75 seconds.
Format: 1920×1080, 16:9, 30 FPS

## SCENE 01 — THE PERSON

Start with the junior IT Support employee during a normal workday.
Keep the visual environment professional, modern, realistic, and minimal.
Do not make him look like a stock-photo employee.
Show him receiving a device handover request.
At first, everything looks normal.
On-screen text: "Another device handover."
Pause.
Then: "Another manual process."

## SCENE 02 — BEFORE AUTOMATION

Follow the employee through the old process.
Visually show the sequence:
Paper Form → Signature → Email → Follow-up → Manual System Update → File / Record → Another Email → Repeat
The employee should gradually become surrounded by repetitive tasks.
Use subtle visual multiplication: more emails, more paper, more notifications, more follow-ups.
Do not exaggerate this into comedy.
The feeling should be: repetitive, inefficient, fragmented, time-consuming.
Use small floating contextual pop-ups such as:
"Manual" "Repeated Daily" "Paper-Based" "Multiple Follow-ups" "High Manual Effort" "Risk of Human Error"
These pop-ups should feel like premium UI annotations rather than PowerPoint labels.

## SCENE 03 — THE PAIN

Slow the video slightly.
Show the employee looking at the repeated process.
On-screen copy:
"The task wasn't difficult."
Then: "But repeating it every day was costing time."
Then: "And every manual step created another opportunity for error."
Show subtle popup indicators:
TIME ↓ Lost to repetitive work
EFFORT ↑ Manual handling
RISK ↑ Human error
RECORDS Fragmented

## SCENE 04 — THE QUESTION

Clear the screen.
Give this scene visual breathing room.
Show the employee stopping and looking at the process differently.
Large centered statement:
"Why are we still doing this manually?"
This should be one of the strongest moments in the video.
Do not rush this scene.

## SCENE 05 — THE UNEXPECTED PART

Reveal: "He was a junior IT Support employee."
Pause.
Then: "He had no previous automation experience."
Make this reveal important.
Then show his mindset shifting from frustration to curiosity.
Animate these words one by one: LEARN. EXPERIMENT. BUILD. IMPROVE.
Supporting text: "He started learning what he needed to solve the problem himself."
Do not portray him as an expert developer.
Show learning, testing, iteration, and curiosity.

## SCENE 06 — BUILDING THE SOLUTION

Visually transform the messy old process into a clean digital workflow.
Do not use dense architecture diagrams.
Keep it simple and intuitive.
Represent the new workflow as:
SUBMIT ↓ TRIGGER ↓ REVIEW ↓ GENERATE RECORD ↓ CENTRALIZE ↓ NOTIFY
Also visually show multiple sites feeding into one centralized process.
Use elegant line animation and motion.
The viewer should understand the workflow within a few seconds without needing technical knowledge.

## SCENE 07 — AFTER AUTOMATION

Return to the SAME employee. This is important.
Show the same situation from Scene 01, but now the experience is completely different.
Before: paper, emails, manual follow-ups, manual updates, fragmented records.
After: digital submission, automated flow, centralized record, automatic notifications, clean process.
Use either a cinematic before/after transition OR a brief split-screen comparison.
Make the difference visually obvious.

## SCENE 08 — IMPACT POP-UPS

Now introduce business impact.
Pop-ups should appear naturally around the employee/process. For example:
TIME SAVED [X]% / [X hours]
MANUAL STEPS [X] → [X]
PAPER Reduced / Eliminated
PROCESSING TIME [Before] → [After]
SITES [X] connected
RECORDS Centralized
ERROR RISK Reduced
MANUAL EMAILS Reduced
IMPORTANT: Do NOT invent any quantitative metrics.
Create editable placeholders for metrics that can be replaced later with verified values.
If no verified number exists, use qualitative wording such as:
"Less Manual Work" "Faster Processing" "Better Accuracy" "Centralized Records" "Improved Accountability" "One Consistent Process" "Reduced Repetitive Work"
Do not show all pop-ups at once.
Sequence them elegantly as the story progresses.

## SCENE 09 — THE HUMAN OUTCOME

Return attention to the employee.
The story is not: "We implemented automation."
The story is: "He experienced a problem and decided to improve it."
Use: "He wasn't asked to build it."
Pause.
"He saw a problem."
Then: "And found a better way."

## SCENE 10 — FINAL MESSAGE

Transition from MANUAL to AUTOMATED.
Then final copy: "No previous automation experience."
Pause.
"Just curiosity, ownership, and the drive to improve."
END CARD:
WE FOUND A BETTER WAY.
Story 01
Corporate Technology
Optionally include: "Small improvements can create meaningful impact."

## VISUAL STYLE

Cinematic · Editorial · Premium · Modern · Human · Minimal · Technology-driven without looking futuristic · Similar to a premium brand film rather than an internal corporate presentation.
Use: strong typography, clean layouts, subtle Elm-inspired orange accents, neutral light or soft dark backgrounds where appropriate, smooth camera movement, elegant motion graphics, high-quality UI overlays, depth and composition, sophisticated transitions, controlled animation timing, visual contrast between BEFORE and AFTER.

**BEFORE visual language:** slightly more crowded and fragmented — more visual layers, more interruptions, slightly faster notification movement, overlapping tasks, repeated elements. Do NOT make the employee look miserable or incompetent. The problem is the process, not the person.

**AFTER visual language:** calmer, cleaner, more structured, faster, easier, controlled. Smoother motion and more whitespace. The viewer should visually feel the efficiency improvement.

**Pop-up design:** modern product UI or data overlays (e.g. `┌ TIME SAVED +XX%`, `┌ PROCESSING TIME XX min → XX min`, `┌ MANUAL STEPS XX → XX`, `┌ RECORDS CENTRALIZED`). Keep them minimal. Avoid large cards or dashboard-looking widgets. The metrics should complement the story, not dominate it.

## STRICTLY AVOID

AI glow, neon gradients, robots, brains, neural networks, futuristic holograms, generic AI graphics, generic stock people, cartoon characters, 3D corporate characters, excessive icons, generic "digital transformation" visuals, PowerPoint-style diagrams, dense text, long bullet lists, technical screenshots, Microsoft product logos as the hero, cheesy success animations, confetti, excessive transitions.
Do not make the solution feel like an advertisement for Microsoft Forms, Excel, Outlook, or Power Automate. The employee and his mindset are the focus.

## TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

React + Remotion · 1920×1080 · 16:9 · 30 FPS · ≈60–75 s · reusable scene components · reusable typography components · reusable metric pop-up component · centralized configuration file for all text · centralized configuration for impact metrics · easy ability to replace employee name, title and photo later · smooth scene transitions · clean timeline structure · CSS/SVG animation where possible · no paid assets · no copyrighted stock footage · content editable for future stories in the campaign.

Design this as the FIRST story in a larger Corporate Technology storytelling campaign. Future stories (different employees, problems, technologies, outcomes) follow the same DNA:
PERSON → PROBLEM → INITIATIVE → BETTER WAY → IMPACT
